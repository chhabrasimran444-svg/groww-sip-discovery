Why do first-time investors stop their SIPs?

The engine read 31,353 public reviews, forum posts, Reddit threads and YouTube comments, kept the 899 that describe someone's own SIP, and labelled each against a codebook built around one metric: the share of first-time investors with an active SIP 12 months after their first one.

Check any finding yourself

Every number and answer here traces back to a real public post. A reviewer can follow any claim to its source in a few clicks.

  1. Pick a claim. For example "most SIPs that ended were not ended by choice" in the Dashboard, or any answer in Ask Assistant.
  2. Open the posts behind it. In Ask Assistant, click a citation such as k18 to see the post's evidence phrase, theme and source. In Dashboard, use Every labelled post: filter by theme or drop signal and search the text.
  3. Read the original. Click the source name. Reddit and forum links open the exact thread. YouTube links open the video, and Play Store links open the Groww app page, where single reviews or comments can't be linked: search for the quoted phrase there.
  4. Check the label against the codebook. Compare the post with the theme definitions below. Every evidence phrase was checked by code to appear word-for-word in its post.
  5. Re-label it independently. Paste the post into the Live Analyzer and see whether a fresh label agrees.
  6. Recount the numbers. Download the labelled posts as a spreadsheet. Filter is_drop = TRUE to get the drop signals, or count by drop_mechanism to rebuild "how SIPs ended".
  7. Check how far the labels can be trusted. Download the golden-set comparison to see each of the 25 posts with the researcher's label, the engine's label and whether they agreed. The AI Evidence Log explains the failures and the three corrections the researcher made.

How the engine works

  1. Collect. Play Store reviews, TradingQ&A and Upstox threads and YouTube comments through a Colab notebook; Reddit through an Apify run. A SIP keyword filter (English and Hindi) keeps candidates. Emails, PAN, phone and account numbers are masked and usernames are never stored.
  2. Screen. Each unique post is read once: does it describe the writer's own SIP situation? Advice to others, news and polls are screened out.
  3. Label. Each relevant post gets a main theme, secondary themes, journey stage, what they did, how the SIP ended, the trigger before it, severity, emotion, app area and segment hints, backed by a verbatim phrase from the post. The trigger is kept apart from the cause, so "the market fell" never stands in for why someone stopped.
  4. Validate. Code forces every label into the codebook's values and checks that each phrase exists in the post. A blind golden set compares the labels with the researcher's own.
  5. Quantify and answer. Themes are ranked by opportunity score, continuers are compared with drop-offs, needs are grouped and counted. The assistant answers only from these posts and cites them.

Labels for the 899 posts were applied by Claude in a working session against the written codebook. The Ask Assistant and Live Analyzer call Claude from this page, on the viewer's own Claude account.

Where the engine failed

  • Neighbouring themes. "Pause button disabled" was coded Can't flex the SIP; the researcher chose App glitches. The codebook had no tie-break rule.
  • A missing theme. Switching from regular to direct plans was forced into Moving SIPs to another platform.
  • Literal reading. Complaints from current investors were coded "Not mentioned" instead of Struggling but continuing.
  • First-time status. The engine left it Unclear unless stated; the researcher inferred it from tone.
  • Relevance edges. App complaints mixing trading and mutual funds were screened out; the researcher kept them.
