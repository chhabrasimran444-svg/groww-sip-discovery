How the engine works

  1. Collect. Play Store reviews, TradingQ&A and Upstox threads and YouTube comments through a Colab notebook; Reddit through an Apify run. A SIP keyword filter (English and Hindi) keeps candidates. Emails, PAN, phone and account numbers are masked and usernames are never stored.
  2. Screen. Each unique post is read once: does it describe the writer's own SIP situation? Advice to others, news and polls are screened out.
  3. Label. Each relevant post gets a main theme, secondary themes, journey stage, what they did, how the SIP ended, the trigger before it, severity, emotion, app area and segment hints, backed by a verbatim phrase from the post. The trigger is kept apart from the cause, so "the market fell" never stands in for why someone stopped.
  4. Validate. Code forces every label into the codebook's values and checks that each phrase exists in the post. A blind golden set compares the labels with the researcher's own.
  5. Quantify and answer. Themes are ranked by opportunity score, continuers are compared with drop-offs, needs are grouped and counted. The assistant answers only from these posts and cites them.

Labels for the 899 posts were applied by Claude in a working session against the written codebook. The Ask Assistant and Live Analyzer call Claude from this page, on the viewer's own Claude account.

Summary

The engine read 3,207 unique public posts about SIPs and labelled the 899 relevant ones against a written codebook. Every label is backed by a verbatim phrase that code confirmed exists in the post. You can test it in the Groww SIP Discovery Engine (this site: ask questions, browse findings, label new posts live); source-level charts are in the engine's Dashboard tab.

Three findings hold up under the blind check (67–78% agreement at this level):

  • Drop-offs split evenly between machinery and understanding. Of 338 posts showing someone stopping, pausing or struggling, 145 trace to operations (autopay, app glitches, fund suspensions, pause limits, no support) and 151 to expectations or misconceptions. Only 32 trace to money pressure.
  • Most SIPs that ended were not chosen to end. Of 124 posts that say how a SIP ended, 72 ended without the investor deciding to stop: cancelled by the fund or platform (36), a failed or lapsed payment (29), or a pause never resumed (7).
  • Market falls are a trigger, not a cause. They appear in 99 posts, and 54 of those people are still investing.

How far to trust it: the broad findings above are reasonably solid. Exact theme labels on single posts are not (28% exact agreement with the researcher, blind), so the dashboard reads themes in aggregate, never post by post. The researcher's corrections were then applied to all posts as codebook v5 (see Golden set).

How the engine works

The engine runs in five steps. Code does the collecting, checking and counting, and Claude reads and labels each post against a written codebook.

  1. Collect. A Colab notebook pulls Play Store reviews, forum threads and YouTube comments. An Apify run pulls Reddit. A keyword filter keeps posts that mention SIPs, and emails, PAN, phone and account numbers are replaced with placeholders. Usernames are never stored.
  2. Screen. Every unique post is read once for relevance: does it describe the writer's own SIP situation? Advice to others, news and polls are screened out. 899 of 3,207 posts were kept.
  3. Code. Each relevant post gets a main theme and up to three secondary themes, plus journey stage, what they did with the SIP, how it ended, the trigger before the change, severity (0–3), emotion, the app area involved, segment hints (income, age, money source, influence), and free text for unmet need, expectation and behaviour change. Each label set must quote a verbatim phrase from the post.
  4. Validate. Code forces every label into the codebook's allowed values and checks that each quoted phrase exists in the post. A blind golden set compares the labels with the researcher's own.
  5. Quantify. Code ranks themes by an opportunity score (reach × link to drop-off × √severity × addressability), compares continuers with drop-offs, splits by source and first-time investors, and counts recurring unmet needs. Every number in the dashboard and in this log is computed from the labelled posts.

Who labelled the posts: Claude, in a chat session, working through the posts in batches against the codebook. There was no API key or paid model run. This is stated on the dashboard too. The recurring unmet needs were grouped by Claude and counted by code.

The code (collectors, labelling validator, evaluation, analysis) is in the project files: collect.py, collect_reddit_apify.py, build_from_codes.py, golden.py, analysis.py, export_dashboard.py.

Codebook and how it changed

The codebook (taxonomy.yaml) has 31 themes and 23 decision rules. Each theme records which metric leak it feeds and how addressable it is in-product (0–5, without recommending funds or using fear). Its central rule is that the trigger and the cause are coded separately, so "the market fell" never stands in for why someone stopped. All 899 posts carry the final version (v5).

Golden set: researcher vs engine

On 25 blind-labelled posts, the researcher and the engine agreed on relevance 80% of the time and on the broad drivers 67–78%, but on the exact main theme only 28%.

How the set was built. 25 posts were drawn at random before Reddit was added: 20 the engine had screened in and 5 it had screened out (Play Store 6, TradingQ&A 7, Upstox 4, YouTube 8). The researcher labelled them in a spreadsheet without seeing the engine's labels, using the same codebook options. Detail fields are compared only on the 18 posts both judged relevant. The blind scores below are the honest measure. After them, the researcher's three corrections were turned into codebook v5 rules and applied to all 899 posts; the same 25 posts were then re-scored. That second column is optimistic, because these posts informed the fixes, so it is reported beside the blind score, never instead of it.

Where they failed, and why:

  • Neighbouring themes with no tie-break rule. For "SIP pause button is disabled" the researcher chose App glitches and the engine chose Can't flex the SIP (with App glitches as secondary). The same happened with an AMC SIP amount change and a "15 years, who has seen that" stop. Counting secondary themes lifts agreement from 28% to 44%.
  • A missing theme forced a near-miss. Two posts about switching from regular to direct plans were coded by the engine as Moving SIPs to another platform. The researcher coded them as charges or fund doubt. The codebook had no theme for plan type at the time (v5 added one).
  • "What they did" read literally vs by intent. In 7 of the 12 disagreements the researcher chose Struggling but continuing for a complaint from a current investor. The engine chose Not mentioned or Continuing because the post doesn't say what they did with the SIP.
  • First-time status inferred vs stated. In 10 of the 12 disagreements the researcher inferred Likely or No from tone, where the engine followed the rule "Unclear unless stated". In the other 2, both committed but differed by one step. That rule leaves 60% of posts Unclear, which limits the first-timer cut.
  • Relevance at the edges. The engine screened out two app complaints that mix trading with mutual funds and one reply giving advice to another investor. The researcher kept them. The researcher screened out "i just started sip" and a newcomer's comment on platform fees, which the engine kept.

What changed because of the golden set. Three of the five failure patterns had a clear fix the researcher's labels supported, so they became codebook v5 rules and were applied to every post, not just the 25 checked. 74 posts changed, and each change is logged in the engine's downloadable change file (Method & evidence tab).

  • New theme for plan type: 31 posts moved to Regular vs direct plans and commissions from platform switch, trust or charges. This also surfaced a Groww-specific pain point: 9 Play Store reviews about a paid plan that routes investments into regular funds.
  • Struggling for current investors with a SIP problem: 42 posts moved from Not mentioned to Struggling.
  • Misinformation as news noise: the false 5.41% expense-ratio post.

The headline findings barely moved: drop signals now split 145 machinery and 151 understanding (was 147 and 146), and the top of the opportunity ranking is unchanged. The other two patterns, a tie-break between neighbouring themes and first-time status from tone, need a fresh blind sample to test fairly and are the next iteration.

Where I rejected or corrected the AI

In three places I overruled the engine's label after comparing it with my own blind labels. Each one changes how a finding should be read.

1. A false expense-ratio claim was coded as a real charge. A YouTube comment said the Motilal Oswal Midcap direct fund's expense ratio had jumped to 5.41%. The engine coded it Confusion about tax, exit load or charges, taking the number at face value. I changed it to Market news and crash headlines. The claim is false: Value Research and Zerodha Coin both list the direct plan's expense ratio at 0.65% (checked 28 October 2026). What drives this investor is misinformation that creates panic, not a charge they misunderstand. Why it matters: posts like this belong under noise that triggers drop-offs, and a verified fund-facts view in the app answers them without fear or urgency.

2. Switching to direct plans was coded as leaving the platform. Two posts asked how to move from regular to direct plans after investing through an advisor. The engine coded them Moving SIPs to another platform. I rejected that: these investors aren't leaving, they want to stop paying commission, so I coded charges and fund doubt. The codebook had no theme for plan type, so the engine forced the nearest one. Why it matters: these posts overstated platform-switch counts, which is why v5 added a theme for plan type.

3. Complaints from current investors were coded "Not mentioned" instead of "Struggling". For posts like "money locked, no funds allotted" or "app not showing correct MF balances", the engine said the post doesn't state what the person did with the SIP. I chose Struggling but continuing: someone with an active SIP whose money is stuck is still investing and finding it hard, which is the codebook's own definition. Why it matters: a literal reading hides at-risk investors under "Not mentioned" and undercounts the group most likely to lapse.

All three corrections were then written into the codebook as v5 rules and applied to every post, not only the ones I labelled: 74 posts changed in total (see What changed because of the golden set).

Limitations and next iteration

The biggest limits are who posts in public and how reliable single-post themes are. The next run should fix the codebook gaps found above and re-test on a fresh blind sample.

Limitations

  • Public posts skew to people with a problem or a question. Shares describe what people say in public, not Groww's user base.
  • Only 112 of 899 relevant posts are about Groww itself. Industry-wide patterns are useful, but Groww-specific claims rest on the Play Store reviews.
  • No App Store reviews, and no automated social media. Income, age and money source are unstated in at least 94% of posts, so segment comparisons are thin.
  • Labels came from Claude in a chat session, not a fixed model run. The code for an API-based run exists (classify.py), but this dataset was not produced by it.
  • The golden set is small (25 posts) and drawn before Reddit was added. Its figures could move by ±10–20 points on another sample.

Next iteration

  1. Add the two rules not yet applied: a tie-break between neighbouring themes (for example, a feature that exists but misbehaves is App glitches; a feature that doesn't exist is Can't flex the SIP), and when first-time status may be inferred as Likely.
  2. Draw a fresh blind set of 20–25 posts, including Reddit, for the researcher, and report v5 and v6 agreement on it. The original 25 are now development data and are not reused as a test.
  3. Run 8–10 interviews with first-time investors to fill the gaps public posts can't: why they started, how often they check, and income stability.
  4. Before building a product fix, validate the top two opportunities (autopay recovery and flexing instead of stopping) against Groww's own SIP lapse data.
